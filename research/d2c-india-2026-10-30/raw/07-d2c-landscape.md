# State of Indian D2C, 2025-2026 (sweep file 07)

Prepared 30 Oct 2026. Coverage is partial: several searches were rate-limited, three source sites did not resolve from this session (cbre.co.in, cxotoday.com, indianretailer.com), and the primary Redseer and Bain reports were not reachable. Anything marked "snippet only" or "unverified" was taken from search-result text, not from a fetched page.

Rating key: A = official/filing, B = reputable press or named research house, C = blog, vendor, aggregator, or YouTube.

## Key takeaways

1. Indian D2C funding has recovered from its 2024 low but is still well below the 2022 peak. Tracxn (Aug 2026 report, via press) puts 2025 at about $898M, up 9% YoY, against a peak of about $1.6B in 2022. [B, Tracxn via TechNode, 26 Aug 2026]
2. The 2024 base figure is contested: $757M (Tracxn, Apr 2025), $824M (Tracxn, Aug 2026), and $595M (undated source). Use a range, not one number. [B]
3. Exits now outnumber new funding events in the story: Tracxn counts 15 D2C IPOs and 105 acquisitions from 2021 to Aug 2026, against about $6B raised across roughly 2,000 rounds. [B, TechNode 26 Aug 2026]
4. Consolidators and conglomerates are the main buyers. Wipro Consumer Care and USV India completed D2C acquisitions in H1 2026. HUL's $350M buy of Minimalist (Jan 2025) is the largest disclosed deal in the Tracxn set. [B]
5. Listed D2C names are mixed. Meesho listed 10 Dec 2025 at Rs 162 (46% premium to the Rs 111 issue price), then fell after the 7 Jan 2026 lock-in expiry. Lenskart's Q4 FY26 revenue rose about 46% YoY while reported profit fell about 7.5%. FirstCry's Q3 FY26 loss widened about 160% YoY to Rs 38.4 Cr. [B, Business Standard, Inc42]
6. Honasa (Mamaearth) is the clearest profitability story in the listed set: Q1 FY27 (quarter to June 2026) net profit reported at about Rs 90.4 Cr, up 119%, with EBITDA margin near 14% versus about 7.7% a year earlier. Revenue figures differ by source (Rs 756 Cr vs Rs 785 Cr like-for-like). Check the NSE filing. [B, YourStory Aug 2026; A to verify, NSE filing 13 Aug 2026]
7. Paid acquisition is getting more expensive. One 2026 source reports average Meta CAC of Rs 502 versus Rs 380 in 2025 (+32%). Other vendor blogs cite CPM rises of 40-60% since 2023. Directionally consistent, but none is a primary dataset. [C]
8. Growth is moving to smaller cities and to repeat-purchase categories. Unicommerce says about 66% of new D2C orders in FY26 came from Tier 2 and 3 cities, and ClickPost says skincare, wellness, new-age fashion, pet care and sleep products make up about 82% of D2C orders in its 62-brand sample. [B/C, Mediabrief Unicommerce; Indian Retailer ClickPost]
9. Channel mix: marketplaces still dominate online retail (about 64% share per one 2025 trade piece). D2C sites are roughly 15% of online retail sales by one estimate, and a 20:80 D2C-to-marketplace/quick-commerce split is cited as typical. Quality of these numbers is low. [C]
10. Offline is growing for D2C. CBRE (H1 2026, snippet only) says D2C brands took about 28% of India's retail leasing, up from 23% a year earlier, with fashion about 60% of that space. CBRE also says brands now prioritise store-level profitability over raw outlet count. [B, CBRE via search snippet]
11. Investors are screening on contribution margin after marketing (CM2) and channel-level P&L, not on top-line growth. Reported valuations compress to about 2-4x revenue. These are blog-sourced claims. [C]
12. Owned-audience brands report lower repeat-purchase costs than marketplace-first brands, which are still searching for profitability. [B, YourStory Feb 2026, snippet]
13. Startup distress is broad: Tracxn counts 11,223 startup shutdowns in India through Oct 2025 (all sectors), about 30% above 2024's 8,649. D2C-specific shutdown or down-round data for 2026 was not found. [B, Financial Express via search snippet; URL not captured]

## Findings by sub-question

### 1. Market size and forecasts

- The definitions diverge so much that no single "market size" figure is reliable.
- Redseer (secondary citation only): about $30-35B GMV by 2027, with roughly 40% CAGR 2022-27 cited from a Business Standard article. Primary Redseer report not reached. [B secondary, via echai.ventures, undated; https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-category-research/how-big-is-my-category-in-india]
- Mordor Intelligence: USD 87.5B in 2025, USD 108.76B in 2026, USD 322.1B by 2031 (24.3% CAGR). Definition likely broader (all D2C e-commerce). [C, via GII Research listing, https://www.giiresearch.com/report/moi2125430-india-d2c-e-commerce-market-share-analysis.html; date not shown]
- McKinsey (via Apparel Resources): D2C sales about US$10-11B today, rising to US$55-60B by 2030, about 38% CAGR. [B secondary, https://apparelresources.com/business-news/retail/tier-2-3-cities-drive-next-wave-growth-indias-d2c-sector/; date not shown]
- Inc42 "D2C 3.0": D2C GMV from about $65B to $310B, about 37% CAGR, versus 22% for all e-commerce (retail GMV share by 2031). [B, https://inc42.com/features/d2c-3-0-the-new-rules-of-indias-d2c-economy/; date not shown]
- Kashmir Reader (Feb 2026) attributes a McKinsey projection of D2C at about twice a USD 25-30B MSME-focused segment by 2030. [B secondary, https://kashmirreader.com/2026/02/26/indias-d2c-e-comm-to-grow-3x-faster-hit-usd-60-billion-by-2030-report/]
- ITLN: D2C brands at a $60B industry by FY27. [C/B secondary, https://www.itln.in/e-commerce/d2c-brands-estimated-to-become-a-60-billion-industry-by-fy27-report-1345806; date not shown]
- Bain: no D2C market-size estimate found.
- Pattern: the 2026 consensus is about 35-40% CAGR for D2C, but the 2025 base ranges from roughly $10B to $87B depending on definition. Use only with the definition stated.

### 2. Funding trends

- Tracxn (Aug 2026, via press): about $6B equity across about 2,000 rounds, Jan 2021 to Aug 2026. Annual round counts stayed between 307 and 380. [B, https://technode.global/2026/08/26/indian-d2c-companies-raised-6b-in-equity-funding-across-2000-rounds-between-2021-and-2026-tracxn/]
- 2025: about $898M, up 9% YoY, after 2024's trough. [B, same URL, and https://india.entrepreneur.com/business-news/indias-d2c-sector-raised-usd-6b-since-2021-tracxn-report]
- 2024 conflicts: $757M, down 18% from $930M in 2023 (Tracxn, Apr 2025). [B, https://www.business-standard.com/amp/companies/start-ups/india-s-d2c-segment-sees-funding-decline-to-757-million-in-2024-125041601070_1.html, 16 Apr 2025] vs $824M (Tracxn Aug 2026, revised). [B] vs $595M (undated). [C]
- Stage mix: early-stage bets rose while D2C total funding fell in 2024. [B, https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/early-stage-bets-rise-even-as-d2c-funding-falls-18-in-2024-tracxn-report-62558.htm, early 2025]
- Late-stage funding fell 69% in value between 2022 and 2025. [B secondary, CXOToday snippet; page not fetched, host unresolved; date not shown]
- Tracxn India Tech Annual Funding Report 2025: total Indian tech funding about $10.5B, down 17%. [B secondary, via press, date not shown]
- 2026 YTD D2C funding: not found.
- Pattern: capital is concentrating in fewer, later, more-profitable deals, and early-stage money has shifted to category or channel bets. Partly inferred from the 2024 stage split.

### 5. Profitability shifts (CAC, margins, offline, marketplace dependence)

- CAC: average Meta CAC Rs 502 in 2026 vs Rs 380 in 2025 (+32%). [C, https://startupwired.com/2026/09/10/indian-d2c-startups-making-customer-acquisition-profitable/, 10 Sep 2026]
- CPM and cost trends: Meta CPMs up 40-60% since 2023; cost per acquisition for static creatives more than doubled since 2023. [C, https://upgrowth.in/d2c-performance-marketing-in-india; date not shown]. Digital CPMs rising 20-25%/yr. - Dependence: over 70% of D2C brands rely on Meta as primary acquisition channel. [C, Entrepreneur India study; URL and date not verified]
- Margin benchmarks (differ by source): CM1 50-65%, CM2 28-45%, CM3 8-20%, target above 15% for true unit profit. [C, unverified; https://startupwired.com/2026/09/10/indian-d2c-startups-making-customer-acquisition-profitable/]
- Offline: D2C share of retail leasing 28% in H1 2026, up from 23%. H1 2025: 5.95 lakh sq ft, 18%. Fashion about 60% of that. [B, CBRE via search snippet; https://www.cbre.co.in/press-releases/d2c-brands-continue-to-deepen; host unresolved; date H1 2026, release date not confirmed]
- Offline lowers CAC and returns: widely repeated, thinly evidenced. [C]
- Quick commerce: over 30% of quick-commerce sales from new-age brands; nearly 2,000 new dark stores in FY24 (Blume-sourced). [C, via search snippet; source URL not captured; FY24 data is dated]. brnd.me reports quick commerce above 10% of its revenue. ### 8. Channel split: own site vs marketplace vs quick commerce vs offline

- Scaled brands run about five channels (own site, marketplaces, quick commerce, social commerce, offline). Own site carries first-party data and repeat purchase; marketplace and quick commerce act as paid reach. ## Ranking / visibility mechanics

Only one claim here is sourced, and it is low-confidence. The rest is inference and is labelled.

- FACT [C]: marketplace search and quick commerce grid placement are paid-influenced. Vendor blogs say so. No platform document on ranking was reached in this sweep.
- PATTERN (inferred): on Amazon and Flipkart, a new brand gets early visibility from paid ads and reviews, so paid acquisition costs are the entry price. Repeat purchase and review velocity then sustain rank. This is the logic behind owned-audience and offline-trust arguments above. Not verified against Amazon Seller Central documentation; verify before acting.
- PATTERN: offline and retail store space helps trust, which lowers blended CAC and returns. Supported only by qualitative commentary.

## What a small new D2C seller should do / avoid (pattern, labelled)

Pattern, based on the facts above. Not a sourced recommendation.

- Do: build a repeat-purchase product (replenishable, high-frequency) before scaling paid acquisition. The category data favour this.
- Do: track CM2 per channel and per order from the start. Investors and funders now ask for it.
- Do: treat marketplace sales as paid reach, not as the owned customer base. Capture first-party data where possible.
- Do: test Tier 2 and 3 demand. Growth is shifting there, per Unicommerce.
- Avoid: relying on Meta alone. Concentration on one paid channel is exposed to CPM and CAC inflation (above 70% of D2C brands rely on Meta per one unverified study).
- Avoid: assuming offline stores lower CAC without measuring it. The benefit is widely asserted but not measured in these sources.
- Avoid: planning around a 2021-style valuation or roll-up exit. Exits are now mainly to strategic buyers at lower multiples.
